# DataGlass Brand & Positioning Skill

**Read this before you write a single word of DataGlass copy.** This is the senior brand POV — what we are, what we're not, how we sound, and the strategic decisions about when to use English versus Thai. Every sentence published under the DataGlass name should pass the rules in this document.

---

## 0. The cardinal principle

> **Easy on the surface. Rigorous underneath.**
> **Actions are the interface. Math is the proof. Deploy is the payoff.**

This is the master rule. Every other rule serves it.

A seller should never need to understand Mathematical Optimization to use DataGlass. They should wake up, read three Actions, click Deploy, and trust that the math underneath has earned the right to make those recommendations. The website's job is to make the product feel *easy to use tomorrow* — and then to make the math feel *hard to replicate* once the seller looks closer.

**Information architecture that follows from this principle:**

1. **Action first** — what should the seller do?
2. **Profit reason** — why does that matter to their bottom line?
3. **Risk guardrail** — what bounds keep this safe?
4. **Math proof** — what method backs the recommendation (optional, expandable)

If a piece of copy reverses this order, rewrite it.

The brand should make a seller think, in order:
1. *"This looks easy enough to use tomorrow."*
2. *"These people clearly know the math behind marketplace profit."*

## 1. The brand sentences — what goes where

DataGlass has two canonical sentences with different jobs. **Do not confuse them.**

### 1A — The public-facing H1 (benefit-led, not category-led)

The homepage H1 is the seller's first promise. It leads with **pain → outcome**, not with category language.

**EN canonical H1:**
> **Stop guessing. Start making profitable marketplace decisions.**

**Thai canonical H1:**
> **หยุดเดา ตัดสินใจบน Marketplace ที่เพิ่มกำไรได้จริง**

The H1 **does not need to say** "Decision Engine." The category spine still exists — but in the subhero, proof line, comparison pages, and deck.

### 1B — The formal category statement (decks, decks, comparison pages, partner briefings)

When you need a single line that names the category, use this:

**EN canonical:**
> **DataGlass is the Decision Engine that uses Mathematical Optimization to turn marketplace data into profit-ranked Actions sellers can Deploy in one click.**

**Thai canonical:**
> **DataGlass คือ Decision Engine ที่ใช้ Mathematical Optimization เปลี่ยนข้อมูลร้าน marketplace ให้เป็น Actions ที่จัดอันดับตามโอกาสเพิ่มกำไร และ Deploy ได้ในคลิกเดียว**

**Use this category sentence in:** investor decks, sales decks, comparison pages, partner briefings, About / Company, first proof strip on pages where category language adds clarity.
**Do not force it into:** every homepage H1.

### 1C — Proof + safety follow-up (one line after either)

> **Every Action is backed by Mathematical Modeling and checked by Risk-Aware Optimization before Deploy.**
> **ทุก Action มี Mathematical Modeling รองรับ และผ่าน Risk-Aware Optimization ก่อน Deploy**

We are **not** a dashboard. We are **not** an ERP. We are **not** another AI chatbot. We are the decision layer that decides what to do next, and the deploy layer that does it.

## 2. The category spine we own

DataGlass is built on **four linked ideas**. They are not equal — they have different jobs and different placements.

| Phrase | Function | Primary home |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision Engine** | *What we are.* The public-facing category. | Hero, header, pricing, comparison tables, sales decks |
| **Mathematical Optimization** | *How we choose the best Action under constraints.* | Subhero, model strip, solutions, compare, sales decks |
| **Mathematical Modeling** | *How we represent the shop's real economics.* | Foundations, model explainer, technical FAQ, research |
| **Risk-Aware Optimization** | *How we keep every Action inside risk guardrails.* | Risk pillar, enterprise, automation, technical FAQ |

The public-facing category is **Decision Engine.**
The mathematical proof is **Mathematical Optimization**, supported by Mathematical Modeling.
The safety promise is **Risk-Aware Optimization.**

**Do not show all four at once unless the reader is on a Foundations, Research, or enterprise page.** Use progressive disclosure.

These four phrases are **always English** — translating them dissolves the category. Each one gets a Thai bridge on first use on a page, then is referred to by its English name thereafter:

| Term | First-use Thai bridge |
|---|---|
| **Decision Engine** | ระบบช่วยตัดสินใจที่บอกว่าควรทำอะไรต่อ |
| **Mathematical Optimization** | คณิตศาสตร์ที่เลือก Action ที่ดีที่สุดภายใต้ข้อจำกัดของร้าน เช่น margin, stock, budget และ risk |
| **Mathematical Modeling** | โมเดลที่คำนวณจากข้อมูลร้านจริง ไม่ใช่ rule สำเร็จรูป |
| **Risk-Aware Optimization** | เพิ่มกำไรโดยไม่ปล่อยให้ความเสี่ยงเกินกรอบ |

### Why Mathematical Optimization sits above Mathematical Modeling

A model can explain the world. An optimization system **chooses what to do**. Since DataGlass sells Actions, the optimization layer is closer to the product than the modeling layer. **Mathematical Optimization is our load-bearing technical phrase.** Mathematical Modeling supports it.

### Critical rule: never translate "Optimization" as เพิ่มประสิทธิภาพ

**เพิ่มประสิทธิภาพ** is too generic — it sounds like every Thai SaaS. Use the English term `Mathematical Optimization` with the Thai bridge above. In body prose where you need a verb, use **ปรับ** (ปรับโฆษณา, ปรับ Listing) — not เพิ่มประสิทธิภาพ.

## 3. The math stack (three layers, three jobs)

DataGlass does not use math as decoration. Each mathematical layer has a specific job:

| Layer | Job | Example output |
|---|---|---|
| **Mathematical Modeling** | Builds the shop's reality: COGS, fees, vouchers, ad spend, stock, demand, margin | True ROAS per SKU, contribution margin per order line, demand forecast |
| **Mathematical Optimization** | Chooses the best Action under constraints | "Cut bid on keyword K by 30%" / "Boost SKU X" / "Join 9.9 with Voucher tier 2" |
| **Risk-Aware Optimization** | Checks whether the Action should ship | margin floor, budget cap, stock constraint, price-change limit, seller approval gates |

**In copy: never explain all three at once unless the reader is on Foundations or Research.** Seller-facing pages lead with the Action; the math stack appears as proof when the seller scrolls deeper.

## 4. What we are NOT (anti-positioning)

A senior brand officer's first rule: **the brand is also defined by what it refuses to be.**

| Category we are not | Who occupies it | Our refusal sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Dashboard / Analytics** | Looker, Tableau, marketplace-native analytics | "DataGlass is not another dashboard. Dashboards show what happened. DataGlass decides what to do next." |
| **Back-office / ERP / OMS** | Zort, Shipnity, BigSeller, Page365 | "We do not stop at recording orders. We optimize the decisions that produce them." |
| **Fulfillment / 3PL** | MyCloud, Akita, Packhai | "We don't move your boxes. We change the math on what you ship." |
| **Chat tool / Inbox aggregator** | Zaapi, Page365 chat | "We don't talk to your customers. We restructure your campaigns." |
| **Managed service / Agency** | Buzzebees, ad agencies | "We are not your agency. We are your decision layer. You stay in control." |
| **Generic AI / Chatbot** | "AI-powered" everything | "We use Mathematical Optimization with named methods — Robust Optimization, Bayesian inference, Causal inference — not generic AI." |
| **Spreadsheet replacement** | Google Sheets, Excel macros | "DataGlass doesn't replace your spreadsheet. It replaces the guesswork your spreadsheet was supposed to settle." |

**Refinements from review:**

- ❌ Don't say "we don't show data" — the product does show diagnostics, breakdowns, metrics.
- ✅ Say "we do not stop at showing data — we turn it into deployable decisions."
- ❌ Don't say "we're not your hands" — the Deploy layer is operational, so this undercuts the promise.
- ✅ Say "we are not your agency. We are your decision layer."

**The corollary:** never use words that belong to those categories as if they were ours. We don't say "ครบวงจร" (ERP language). We don't lead with "แดชบอร์ด" (dashboard language). We don't say "AI ช่วยคุณ" (generic AI hype). We don't say "ระบบหลังบ้าน" (back-office language).

If a sentence could appear on a Zort or Page365 page without changes, it's not a DataGlass sentence. Rewrite.

## 5. The customer transformation

Every marketing surface should imply, evoke, or directly state this arc:

> **Before DataGlass:** You stare at dashboards. You guess. You manually adjust ads on Shopee Seller Center. You don't know if your ROAS is profitable. You over-spend on losers and under-spend on winners. You discover stockouts after they cost you a campaign. Your margin lives in a spreadsheet nobody trusts.

> **After DataGlass:** A queue of profit-ranked **Actions** waits each morning. You read three sentences per action. You click **Deploy**. The system updates Shopee for you. Margin is the optimization target. Risk is bounded by guardrails. You spend the time you got back on the product itself.

## 6. Brand voice — six principles

DataGlass voice is **mathematically literate-but-seller-readable, profit-first, confident-not-defensive, concrete, direct.**

### Principle 1 — Mathematically literate, but seller-readable

We use terms like elasticity, Bayesian inference, and Robust Optimization when they add credibility — but **always connect them to a concrete seller decision in the same breath.** Naked technical terms block the reader; bridged terms accelerate them.

✅ "DataGlass ใช้ Bayesian inference เพื่อเรียนรู้ pattern ของร้านคุณเมื่อข้อมูลยังไม่เยอะพอ"

❌ "เราใช้ระบบคำนวณอัจฉริยะที่ปรับตัวเองได้" (no math named — too vague)

❌ "เราใช้ Bayesian inference, Robust Optimization, Causal inference, CVaR-95 เพื่อ optimize ร้าน" (jargon dump — blocks the reader)

**Authority does not come from using the most technical word possible.** Authority comes from making the decision logic feel specific, grounded, and hard to fake:

- "True ROAS หลังหัก COGS, marketplace fees, Voucher และ ad spend"
- "Action นี้ไม่ผ่าน guardrail เพราะ stock เหลือพอขายได้แค่ 3 วัน"
- "ลด bid keyword ที่กิน margin แต่ไม่สร้าง contribution profit"

The reader who knows these terms should recognize that DataGlass is technically serious. The reader who doesn't should still understand the business benefit from the Thai bridge. **DataGlass should never make the seller feel excluded.**

### Principle 2 — Profit-first, never sales-first

Choose **กำไร** over **ยอดขาย**. But frame profit claims as **modeled, projected, expected, or action-based** — never guaranteed.

✅ "ช่วยหา Actions ที่มีโอกาสเพิ่มกำไร" / "projected profit recovery"
❌ "เพิ่มกำไรแน่นอน 100%" / "เพิ่มยอดขายและกำไร"

### Principle 3 — Confident, not defensive

We state what the system does. We don't beg. ค่ะ / นะ / เลย allowed in testimonials only, never body copy.

### Principle 4 — Concrete, never abstract

Reach for a number, a verb, an action.

### Principle 5 — Sparingly warm in Thai (once per page max)

"เรามาโตไปด้วยกัน!" — only on the free-tier pricing callout. One warm beat per page.

### Principle 6 — Direct, never academic

No ดังจะเห็นได้ว่า / ในแง่ของ / อาทิเช่น / ประการ / ความเป็นเลิศ.

**Tone register:** between *"confident professional operator"* and *"sharp founder-engineer who can explain things simply."*

## 7. Code-switching strategy — three registers, surface-based

The previous "60% Thai / 40% English" rule is retired. Replaced with a three-register palette:

> **Every English term in DataGlass Thai copy has three valid forms. Pick the one that gives the sentence the right modern-tech feel.**

### The three registers

| Register | Example | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **1. English in Latin script** | "Mathematical Optimization", "Decision Engine", "Actions", "Deploy", "voucher" | Brand category terms, acronyms, brand-coined product nouns. Authority register. |
| **2. ทับศัพท์ (Thai transliteration)** | "โมเดล", "ออปไทไมซ์", "ดีพลอย", "ฟีเจอร์", "แดชบอร์ด", "แพลตฟอร์ม", "อัลกอริทึม" | Connective tissue around brand terms. Modern, native-feeling Thai-tech voice. (Note: **Bundle Deal** and **Boost** stay English — they're brand product nouns/actions, like Listing/Voucher/Campaign.) |
| **3. Pure Thai translation** | "คณิตศาสตร์", "ปรับ", "เพิ่มกำไร", "การตัดสินใจ", "ความเสี่ยง" | Verbs of the win, emotional landing, the seller's actual outcome. |

These are not a hierarchy — they are a palette. A single DataGlass sentence often uses all three:

> *"**DataGlass** ใช้ **โมเดล Mathematical Optimization** เพื่อ **เลือก Action ที่เพิ่มกำไร** ของร้านคุณ"*
> Register 1 (DataGlass) + Register 2 (โมเดล) + Register 1 (Mathematical Optimization, Action) + Register 3 (เลือก / เพิ่มกำไร / ของร้านคุณ)

### Why ทับศัพท์ matters

Modern Thai tech sellers read transliterated terms (โมเดล, แดชบอร์ด, ออปไทไมซ์, ดีพลอย, แพลตฟอร์ม, ฟีเจอร์, อัลกอริทึม) as **fluent and modern, not foreign.** Pure-Thai equivalents (โมเดลทางคณิตศาสตร์, แผงควบคุม, ปรับให้เหมาะที่สุด) often sound academic or robotic. Transliteration is the middle register that keeps technical accuracy while sounding like the way Thai sellers actually talk.

When you want a more native, fluent Thai-tech feel without losing the technical signal, **reach for ทับศัพท์**.

### The Thai-bridge rule is recommended, not mandatory

The previous version required a Thai bridge on first use of every Tier 1 / Tier 2 term. **This is now a recommendation, not a tax.**

- ✅ Recommended: bridge on first use when the page is seller-facing and the term is unfamiliar.
- ✅ Acceptable: skip the bridge when the surrounding context makes meaning clear, or when the page is technical (Foundations, Research) and the reader has self-selected.
- ✅ Acceptable: use ทับศัพท์ instead of a bridge — "โมเดล" carries enough meaning without "Mathematical Modeling — โมเดลที่..."

In other words: the writer chooses the register that fits the sentence. "Mathematical Optimization" can stand alone in English when the proof-line context makes it credible. "โมเดล" can stand alone in ทับศัพท์ when the body prose needs to flow naturally.

> DataGlass Thai copy uses Thai for clarity and emotional landing, English for category and authority, and ทับศัพท์ for modern technical flow. The sentence should feel like a Thai founder speaking naturally about a technical product — not a translated SaaS page and not an academic paper.

### Surface-specific guidance

| Surface | Recommended feel | Math density |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero** | Mostly Thai. 1 strong English category term. | Low |
| **Subhero** | Thai explanation + 1–2 math / category terms with bridge. | Low–Medium |
| **Feature cards** | Thai action verbs + English product / metric terms. | Low |
| **Solutions per-slug** | Thai sentence flow + 2–3 named methods with bridges. | Medium |
| **Foundations / research** | More English math terms, each with Thai bridge on first use. | High |
| **Pillars** | Thai pillar names + 1–2 English methods per pillar with bridges. | Medium–High |
| **Pricing** | Thai + plan names in English. | Low |
| **Compare pages** | Thai + 1 trinity term. Anti-positioning in Thai. | Low–Medium |
| **CTA** | Thai, short, canonical. | None |
| **FAQ** | Thai-led. Technical terms get inline bridges. | Variable |
| **Email / nurture** | Thai-led. Category term once, action verbs throughout. | Low–Medium |

### Read-aloud test

Does your Thai sentence sound like a Thai e-commerce founder explaining their product to a fellow founder over coffee? If yes, ship. If it sounds like a translation or an academic paper, rewrite.

## 8. Mathematical vocabulary — tiers and the no-naked-jargon rule

### Tier 1: Category / core proof terms (used freely across surfaces)

- **Decision Engine**
- **Mathematical Optimization**
- **Risk-Aware Optimization**

Always English. Thai bridge on first use is **recommended, not mandatory** — use a bridge when the context is seller-facing and unfamiliar; skip when surrounding context makes meaning clear, or when the surface is technical (Foundations, Research) and the reader has self-selected.

### Tier 1.5: Modeling infrastructure

- **Mathematical Modeling**

Plays a supporting role behind Mathematical Optimization. Lives on Foundations, Model Explainer, technical FAQ, and research. Avoid in hero/subhero unless the page is specifically about how DataGlass represents the shop's economics.

### Tier 2: Credibility methods (use selectively, with bridge + seller decision)

- **Robust Optimization**
- **Bayesian inference**
- **Causal inference**
- **Price elasticity**
- **Monte Carlo simulation**

### Tier 3: Deep technical terms (research / model explainer / expandable)

- **CVaR-95**
- **Latent factor models**
- **Stochastic inventory policy**
- **Dynamic pricing under demand uncertainty**

### The "no naked jargon" rule

**Never name-drop math without immediately tying it to a seller decision.**

Pattern: **English term — short Thai bridge — concrete seller decision.**

❌ Bad: "DataGlass ใช้ Bayesian inference และ Robust Optimization เพื่อเพิ่มประสิทธิภาพร้านค้า"

✅ Better: "DataGlass ใช้ **Bayesian inference** เพื่อเรียนรู้ pattern ของร้านคุณเมื่อข้อมูลยังไม่เยอะพอ และใช้ **Robust Optimization** เพื่อเลือก Actions ที่ยังปลอดภัยเมื่อยอดขายจริงไม่ตรงกับที่คาด"

✅ Best for homepage: "โมเดลเรียนรู้จากข้อมูลร้านคุณ แล้วจัดอันดับ Actions ที่ควร Deploy ก่อน"

## 9. Progressive disclosure — 4-level information architecture

### Level 1 — What the seller sees (hero, feature cards, pricing, CTAs)

Lead with: Actions, profit impact, Deploy, risk guardrails. No deep math.

> "ทุกเช้า DataGlass จัดอันดับ Actions ที่ควรทำก่อน พร้อมเหตุผลสั้น ๆ และปุ่ม Deploy ในคลิกเดียว"

### Level 2 — Why the Action exists (product sections, solutions pages)

> "ระบบไม่ได้ดูแค่ ROAS แต่คำนวณ True ROAS หลังหัก COGS, marketplace fees, Voucher และต้นทุนโฆษณา"

### Level 3 — The math behind it (foundations, model explainer)

Name 2–3 methods. Each with a short Thai bridge.

> "เบื้องหลังคือ Mathematical Optimization ที่เลือก Action ที่เพิ่ม contribution margin มากที่สุด ภายใต้กรอบ margin floor, budget cap และ stock"

### Level 4 — Full technical proof (research, expandable)

CVaR-95, Monte Carlo, latent factors, stochastic policy. Reader has self-selected.

### Homepage math-density allocation (canonical)

- **Hero:** 1 category term. Period.
- **Subhero:** 1 math term, with bridge. Use **Mathematical Optimization**, not Modeling.
- **Product loop:** No deep math. Connect → Decide → Deploy.
- **Trust / foundations strip:** 2–3 named methods, each with short Thai bridge.
- **Research link:** Optional, never blocking.

| Restock alert | เตือนเติมสต็อก | Stockout-risk-driven restock |
| Pause SKU | หยุดขาย SKU | Pause near-stockout or unprofitable SKUs |

All wrapped by the brand verb **Deploy** → "Deploy ในคลิกเดียว."

**Important:** describe Actions as **recommend / prepare with seller approval** unless a workflow is truly autonomous and opt-in. Don't say "auto-join Campaign" — say "แนะนำ Campaign ที่ควรเข้าร่วม และ Deploy หลังผู้ขายอนุมัติ." The Deploy promise is one-click, not zero-click.

### Actions vs action — capitalization

- **Actions** (capital A) = the DataGlass product object / queue. "Actions รายวัน."
- **Action** (capital A, singular) = one specific DataGlass recommendation. "Action นี้แนะนำให้ปรับ Voucher."
- **action** (lowercase) = avoid; reserve for generic prose only.

### Actions in Thai prose — always English, never `การตัดสินใจ`

When EN says "decisions" but means the **DataGlass queue / product object**, TH must keep `Actions` (or singular `Action`) in English. **Do not translate to `การตัดสินใจ` in product contexts.** Examples:

| Wrong (Thai-translated) | Right (Actions stays English) |
|---|---|
| `คิวการตัดสินใจ` | `คิว Actions` |
| `การตัดสินใจรายวัน` | `Actions รายวัน` |
| `จัดอันดับการตัดสินใจ` | `จัดอันดับ Actions` |
| `ทุกการตัดสินใจ` (in product context) | `ทุก Action` |
| `Deploy การตัดสินใจ` | `Deploy Actions` |
| `ส่งการตัดสินใจไปยังร้าน` | `Deploy Actions ไปยังร้าน` |

`การตัดสินใจ` is reserved for **abstract / verb-phrase usage only** — `ในการตัดสินใจว่า...` (in deciding whether), `คณิตศาสตร์ของการตัดสินใจ` (math of decision-making), or the canonical pillar name `การตัดสินใจอัตโนมัติ` (Pillar 2 — Automated Decision Making).

### `True ROAS` — always English, never `ROAS ที่แท้จริง`

`True ROAS` is a defined brand metric with its own glossary page — keep it English in Thai prose. **Never calque it as `ROAS ที่แท้จริง`.** The `True` prefix signals the metric subtracts the costs platform dashboards hide.

⚠️ This does **not** generalize into a `True ___` rule. `true profit` and `true margin` are ordinary descriptive phrases, **not** locked brand metrics — translate them: `true profit` → `กำไรสุทธิ` (formal / calculator) or `เพิ่มกำไรได้จริง` (as proof); `true margin` → `margin หลังหักต้นทุน`. Do not calque as `กำไรที่แท้จริง` / `margin ที่แท้จริง`, and do not write `True Profit` / `True Margin` as if they were English brand metrics. The English label `True Profit Optimization` survives only as the Pillar 1 proper-noun name — its Thai is `เพิ่มกำไรและมาร์จิ้น`.

## 17. Closing direction

DataGlass's job is not to make sellers feel impressed by math. It's to make sellers' Tuesdays better — better ad spend, better Listings, better Campaign joins, better Voucher tiers — and to make sure the math is there, named, and reviewable when a serious seller asks "but why?"

The brand should feel three things at once:

> **Simple to use.** Difficult to replicate. Mathematically serious.

- **Simple to use:** Actions, Deploy, daily queue. The seller doesn't need to study Mathematical Optimization.
- **Difficult to replicate:** Mathematical Optimization, platform-specific decision logic, Risk-Aware Optimization. No competitor has named these methods this clearly for Thai marketplace.
- **Mathematically serious:** named methods, explained in short Thai bridges, with concrete seller decisions attached.

Brand officer's instruction: **never write a sentence that another Thai e-commerce SaaS could write.** When in doubt, name an Action, denominate the win in กำไร (modeled, not guaranteed), and let Mathematical Optimization be the proof that DataGlass earned the right to make that recommendation.

— *DataGlass Brand & Marketing*

## 18. Final Brand Decision (the senior officer's binding ruling)

DataGlass should not lead with technical category language when the seller needs a clear reason to care. The homepage H1 may be benefit-led:

> **Stop guessing. Start making profitable marketplace decisions.**
> **หยุดเดา ตัดสินใจบน Marketplace ที่เพิ่มกำไรได้จริง**

This is the public promise. The category spine still exists underneath:

- **Decision Engine** — what we are
- **Mathematical Optimization** — how we choose the best Action
- **Mathematical Modeling** — how we represent the shop's economics
- **Risk-Aware Optimization** — how we keep every Action inside guardrails

The page order is:

> **Seller pain → profit-first decision → ranked Actions → Deploy in one click → Mathematical Optimization proof → Risk-Aware guardrails**

Never reverse this order on seller-facing pages. Math proves the product; it does not block entry into the product.

DataGlass wins when a seller thinks:

> **"I can use this tomorrow."**

and then, after scrolling:

> **"These people clearly know the math."**

The final brand feeling, in one line:

> **Easy enough to start tomorrow. Serious enough that sellers trust the math. Specific enough that no ERP, dashboard, or AI chatbot can sound the same.**

— *DataGlass Brand & Marketing — Final Direction*
