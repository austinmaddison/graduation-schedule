# Category Positioning — DataGlass

This document is the formal category statement. Use it for investor decks, partnerships, journalist briefings, or any moment where you need to explain in one page what category DataGlass occupies and how we differ from everything else in the Thai e-commerce SaaS market.

The governing principle: **easy on the surface, rigorous underneath.** DataGlass leads with what the seller gets (Actions, Deploy, profit guardrails) and supports it with named methods (Mathematical Modeling, Risk-Aware Optimization, Operations Research, Bayesian inference, Causal inference) — never the reverse.

---

## 1. Category statement

> **DataGlass is the Decision Engine that uses Mathematical Optimization to turn marketplace data into profit-ranked Actions sellers can Deploy in one click.**

Or, in Thai gloss:

> **DataGlass คือ Decision Engine ที่ใช้ Mathematical Optimization เปลี่ยนข้อมูลร้าน marketplace ให้เป็น Actions ที่จัดอันดับตามโอกาสเพิ่มกำไร และ Deploy ได้ในคลิกเดียว**

Proof + safety follow-up (one line after):

> **Every Action is backed by Mathematical Modeling and checked by Risk-Aware Optimization before Deploy.**
> **ทุก Action มี Mathematical Modeling รองรับ และผ่าน Risk-Aware Optimization ก่อน Deploy**

This captures the brand spine: **category → method → data source → output → ease**, followed by **modeling proof → safety promise**.

### Where this category statement belongs

- ✅ **Use for:** investor decks, sales decks, comparison pages, partner briefings, About / Company, first proof strip on pages where category language adds clarity.
- ❌ **Do not force into:** the homepage H1.

The homepage H1 is benefit-led (`Stop guessing. Start making profitable marketplace decisions.` / `หยุดเดา ตัดสินใจบน Marketplace ที่เพิ่มกำไรได้จริง`). The category spine appears in the subhero + proof line + comparison pages + decks. See `SKILL.md §1A vs §1B` for the canonical split.

## 2. The category spine — four linked ideas

DataGlass is built on four linked ideas. They are not equal — they play different roles.

| Phrase | Role |
|---|---|
| **Decision Engine** | What we are. Public-facing category. |
| **Mathematical Optimization** | How we choose the best Action under constraints. The load-bearing technical phrase. |
| **Mathematical Modeling** | How we represent the shop's real economics. Supports Mathematical Optimization. |
| **Risk-Aware Optimization** | How we keep every Action inside guardrails. The safety promise. |

The public-facing category is **Decision Engine**. The mathematical proof is **Mathematical Optimization**, supported by **Mathematical Modeling**. The safety promise is **Risk-Aware Optimization**.

Do not show all four at once unless the reader is on a Foundations, Research, or enterprise page. Use progressive disclosure.

### Decision Engine — *what we are*

A system that produces ranked, deployable actions — not insights, not reports, not alerts. The reader's mental model: "this is the thing that decides what I should do tomorrow." First-use Thai bridge: *"ระบบช่วยตัดสินใจที่บอกว่าควรทำอะไรต่อ — และ Deploy ได้ในคลิกเดียว."*

A Decision Engine ≠ Decision Support System (older category, passive). The "Engine" word is deliberate: it *produces*, it *runs*, it *outputs decisions*. Dashboards display data. Engines produce decisions. We are an engine.

### Mathematical Optimization — *how we choose the best Action*

The load-bearing technical phrase. An optimization system *chooses what to do*. Since DataGlass sells Actions, this is the layer closest to the product spine.

Thai bridge (first use): *"คณิตศาสตร์ที่เลือก Action ที่ดีที่สุดภายใต้ข้อจำกัดของร้าน เช่น margin, stock, budget และ risk"*

**Never translate Optimization as เพิ่มประสิทธิภาพ** — too generic, competitor language. Keep the English term; bridge in Thai with the seller decision attached.

### Mathematical Modeling — *how we represent the shop*

The supporting layer. Mathematical Modeling builds the shop's reality (COGS, fees, vouchers, ad spend, stock, demand, margin) so that Mathematical Optimization has something to optimize over.

Used selectively. Lives on Foundations, Model Explainer, technical FAQ, and research — not in the hero / subhero unless the page is specifically about how DataGlass represents the shop's economics.

### The methods stack (named tools — selectively, with bridges)

- **Operations Research** — the academic discipline
- **Robust Optimization** — variant we emphasize (handles uncertainty)
- **Bayesian inference** — posterior updating from new data
- **Causal inference** — actual lift attribution, not correlation
- **Price elasticity** — demand-response modeling
- **Monte Carlo simulation** — outcome distributions
- **Latent factor models** — demand decomposition (Tier 3, research only)
- **Stochastic methods** — handling uncertainty (Tier 3)

We name these to **signal rigor** — but never naked. Each appears with a Thai bridge and a concrete seller decision attached.

The competitor positioning we deliberately walk past: "AI-powered," "smart system," "intelligent automation." These are buzzwords. We say what we actually use.

### Risk-Aware Optimization — *how we ship*

The promise that every Action is bounded by guardrails:
- **Margin floor** — no Action drops contribution margin below a threshold
- **Budget cap** — no spend recommendation exceeds a ceiling
- **Price-change limit** — pricing moves are bounded per cycle
- **Seller approval gates** — the seller can lock any class of Action
- **Post-deployment measurement** — every deployed Action is measured against expected lift
- **CVaR-95** — Tier 3 risk metric for research/deep-dive surfaces: actions whose 5th-percentile outcome violates the margin floor are not deployed

Risk-Aware Optimization is what lets us automate without scaring the seller. It is the only honest answer to "but what if the system gets it wrong?"

## 3. Anti-positioning matrix (refined)

For each adjacent category, the precise sentence DataGlass uses to refuse to be them.

| Adjacent category | Our refusal sentence |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard / Analytics** | "DataGlass is not another dashboard. Dashboards show what happened. DataGlass decides what to do next." |
| **Back-office / ERP / OMS** | "We do not stop at recording orders. We optimize the decisions that produce them." |
| **Fulfillment / 3PL** | "We don't move your boxes. We change the math on what you ship." |
| **Chat tool / Inbox** | "We don't talk to your customers. We restructure your campaigns." |
| **Managed service / Agency** | "We are not your agency. We are your decision layer. You stay in control." |
| **Generic AI assistant** | "We use specifically Mathematical Optimization with named methods — Robust Optimization, Bayesian inference, Causal inference — not generic AI." |
| **Spreadsheet replacement** | "DataGlass doesn't replace your spreadsheet. It replaces the guesswork your spreadsheet was supposed to settle." |
| **Accounting / financial software** | "We use your accounting truth to optimize your future decisions." |

**Important refinements (from review):**

- ❌ Don't write "we don't show data" — too absolute. The product does show diagnostics, breakdowns, metrics.
- ✅ Use "we do not stop at showing data — we turn it into deployable decisions."
- ❌ Don't write "we're not your hands" — the Deploy layer is operational, so this undercuts the promise.
- ✅ Use "we are not your agency — we are your decision layer."

## 4. The category-creation move

DataGlass is not entering an existing category. We are **creating a new sub-category** for the Thai e-commerce market:

> **"Decision Engine สำหรับผู้ขาย Marketplace ขับเคลื่อนด้วย Mathematical Modeling"**

The closest international analogs are decision-intelligence platforms (Aera, Domino, RapidMiner Decisions) — but none target Thai marketplace sellers, and none ship one-click deploys to Shopee Open Platform / Lazada Open Platform / TikTok Shop Partner API.

The strategic bet: **invest in creating demand for the new category name**, not competing for existing keyword volume. When sellers Google "ระบบจัดการร้านค้าออนไลน์" we lose to Zort/Page365 by definition. When sellers Google "Decision Engine Shopee," we own the result.

## 5. The customer's epiphany (5 beats)

Every marketing surface walks the reader through some subset of:

1. **"My dashboard isn't enough."** — They have data. They still don't know what to do tomorrow.
2. **"My problem isn't visibility. It's decisions."** — They don't need more graphs. They need recommendations.
3. **"DataGlass tells me what decisions to make."** — Concretely. Ranked. With expected lift.
4. **"And I can ship them in one click."** — One-click Deploy is the activation moment.
5. **"And the system has guardrails."** — Risk-Aware Optimization removes the "but what if it's wrong" objection.

Pages target 1–2 of these realizations, not all five. Hero targets (1) + (3). Solutions pages target (3) in detail. Pricing targets (3) → (4). Risk/Pillars targets (5). Compare targets (2).

## 7. What "winning" the category looks like

We win when:
- **Sellers refer to DataGlass by category, not by feature.** "I use a Decision Engine for my Shopee shop."
- **Competitors start using our vocabulary.** When Zort or Page365 says "Decision Engine" or "Mathematical Optimization," we've forced them onto our ground.
- **Investors recognize the category.** "DataGlass is the Decision Engine for Thai e-commerce."
- **The brand scales across SEA.** Decision Engine + Mathematical Modeling translates cleanly to ID, MY, VN, SG. ครบวงจร does not.

We lose when:
- We let ourselves be miscategorized as a dashboard, an ERP, or "another Shopee tool."
- We compete on completeness ("more features than X").
- We translate the trinity — Thai readers stop seeing what we are.
- We adopt market-vendor or accountant-formal voice.
- We push math vocabulary into the first screen and force the seller to study before signup.
